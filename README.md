# Famous Last Words: The Funniest Epitaph

The funniest one-line tombstone epitaph of famous last words:

> I asked for one more minute. They gave me eternity.

51 characters. The setup is the oldest bargain in the book — a dying person asking for just one more minute. The turn is the answer: not a minute, but eternity, granted in full. The joke is the scale of the reply — death doesn't negotiate, it over-delivers, forever. "They gave me eternity" is the whole joke in four words, short enough to carve on a stone and quotable enough to repeat to a friend.

The entry was chosen by an internal pairwise tournament over 16 distinct candidate epitaphs, judged against the competition's published judge brief in order (see CANDIDATES.md for the full bracket and every match). The winner went through the final against the runner-up ("I can see the light. It's just the exit sign."), beating it on the size of the twist and quotability, and beat the semi-finalists ("I never liked funerals. This one included." and "Tell the dog I said goodbye. Tell the cat I said nothing.") on the same criteria.

## How to run

```sh
python3 main.py
```

This writes the epitaph verbatim to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` (default `/output`) and prints it to stdout. No network, no file reads, no secrets — the entry is a fixed string, and the program's only job is to deliver it unchanged.
